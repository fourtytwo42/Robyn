# Liquidity, traction and reward economics

Last verified: 2026-07-20

## Executive conclusion

Robinhood Chain has substantial launch activity, but the specific fee-funded Stock Token reward category remains early. Execution depth is highly concentrated. A system that automatically splits every small fee receipt across arbitrary Stock Tokens will often create poor fills, dust or failed transactions.

The product should therefore accumulate fees, convert only when each basket leg reaches a minimum economic size, use authenticated firm quotes, and retain a failed leg in USDG/WETH rather than silently changing the creator's weights.

## Execution policy

For each immutable basket leg:

1. Accumulate its exact weight in USDG or WETH.
2. Do not quote until the balance exceeds both a configurable dollar threshold and expected gas/API overhead.
3. Request authenticated 0x and 1inch quotes in parallel; optionally compare a directly simulated Uniswap route.
4. Check that the quote's input token, output token, amount, receiver, allowance target and transaction target match the intended operation.
5. Check expiry, minimum output, Chainlink freshness and maximum oracle deviation.
6. Execute atomically; measure actual balance delta rather than trusting return data.
7. On failure, retain that leg's funds and retry later. Never give the failed weight to another asset.
8. Deposit received tokens into the reward controller and increment that asset's cumulative reward-per-share.

Start with at most three assets and restrict the UI's default list to routes that repeatedly pass firm-quote thresholds. The underlying protocol can remain permissionless while the interface communicates “thin route” warnings and requires explicit creator confirmation.

## Fee-flow economics

The fundamental equation is:

`gross reward purchase = trading volume × pool fee × creator share × basket share`

Illustrative capture rates before execution loss:

| Configuration | Gross basket capture as % of volume | $1k daily volume | $10k daily volume | $100k daily volume |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bags advertised maximum creator allocation | 1.00% | $10/day | $100/day | $1,000/day |
| pons advertised creator allocation | 0.70% | $7/day | $70/day | $700/day |
| Alloy advertised model | 0.70% | $7/day | $70/day | $700/day |
| Vladdy 1% tier after 25% platform share | 0.75% | $7.50/day | $75/day | $750/day |
| Vladdy 5% tier after 25% platform share | 3.75% | $37.50/day | $375/day | $3,750/day |
| Robyn planning reference: 1% fee, 75% rewards | 0.75% | $7.50/day | $75/day | $750/day |

The 5% tier funds rewards quickly but taxes every trade heavily. The 1% reference is a more credible starting hypothesis. Actual user behavior must decide it.

Do not market annualized reward/market-cap illustrations as yield or APY. They assume constant daily volume, constant capitalization, no holder turnover, no taxes, no execution failures and no price changes. The JSON model includes 0, 0.5% and 2% execution-loss scenarios solely to make sensitivity visible.

## Batch-size implication

At $1,000 daily volume and a 0.75% capture rate, a three-asset 40/40/20 basket accrues only $3/$3/$1.50 per day. Swapping each leg on every fee event would be irrational. At $10,000 daily volume it accrues $30/$30/$15 daily, still small for thin markets.

Recommended first policy:

- Minimum conversion: the greater of $50 per leg or 10 times the expected end-to-end gas and service cost.
- Maximum delay: 24 hours for liquid approved routes, with the UI showing pending cash.
- Maximum price deviation: begin testing at 100 basis points for liquid names and fail closed; do not hard-code this before quote experiments.
- Maximum basket size: three assets in MVP, five only after gas and transfer-hook profiling.
- Dust: carry forward indefinitely; never socialize it to another weight.

## RPC and operating cost

Browser-direct public RPC is appropriate for wallet reads, transaction submission and moderate polling because load is naturally distributed across user egress addresses. It is not a complete production backend. Robinhood explicitly says its public endpoint is rate-limited and not intended for production-grade, high-throughput or latency-sensitive use.

Use this split:

| Workload | Endpoint |
|---|---|
| Wallet state, balances, recent claims, transaction submission | Browser-direct public RPC, batching, cache, retry/jitter, optional user RPC |
| Complete historical Transfer/event indexing | Project-controlled archive-capable provider |
| Epoch/Merkle construction | Project provider plus independently checked block hashes |
| Keeper execution and preflight simulation | Project provider with public-RPC fallback only for diagnosis |
| Health checks | At least two independent providers |

Current published starting points make this cheap: Alchemy advertises a 30M-CU free tier and QuickNode a 10M-credit trial, but pricing and chain-specific support must be rechecked at procurement. One small protected provider account is enough for MVP correctness-critical tasks; do not proxy all browser traffic or expose its key.

## Go/no-go thresholds before mainnet beta

- At least 90% of intended default assets produce valid authenticated $100 quotes during their trading window.
- Default assets show less than 1% oracle-relative execution loss for $100 conversions in at least 95 of 100 sampled quotes.
- No default leg remains unconvertible for more than five consecutive market sessions in the observation period.
- Median active launch generates enough fees to reach the minimum conversion size at least weekly; otherwise the reward concept feels inert.
- At least 20 creators complete a prototype launch flow and 70% correctly explain that the community token is **not redeemable for the basket**.
- At least 10 real contract-recipient fee claims complete without privileged intervention.

If these fail, ship crypto-only rewards first, reduce the asset list, or pivot to a redeemable basket product with explicit capitalization.
